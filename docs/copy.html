Life Row

This is Life Row.

A place for young men to become, build, and lead.

Introduction

The Foundation.

Mission.

To develop young men who build meaningful futures and become the role models their communities can depend on.

What is Life Row?

Life Row is a planned afterschool community where young men enter as a class, grow together, and graduate together. Our work is shaped by the teachings of Jesus.

Why we exist.

Too many young people face pain, pressure, and difficult decisions without a community consistently involved in their lives. Access to care, constructive outlets, and people who open doors too often depends on what their family can afford.

Who we’re here for.

We welcome East LA’s boys and young men who need people in their corner, including those navigating probation, gang involvement, conflict at home, or uncertainty about their future.

Anger, distrust, and challenging behavior call for patient relationships, clear boundaries, and dependable support. A young man’s hardest moment does not erase his worth or decide who he can become.

What the numbers are.

Violence among youth. In 2020, males accounted for 80% of youth violent-crime arrests, including 92% of murder arrests and 88% of robbery arrests. OJJDP, Trends in Youth Arrests for Violent Crimes.

Jail incarceration. In 2023, males were held in local jails at 343 per 100,000, compared with 56 per 100,000 females—about six times the rate. Bureau of Justice Statistics, Jail Inmates in 2023.

Gang involvement. A national youth study found 11% of boys versus 6% of girls reported having joined a gang. A multicity study found 8.8% versus 7.8%. OJJDP, Gang Prevention: An Overview of Research and Programs.

Structured programs. Randomized evaluations of Chicago’s Becoming a Man program found reductions in violent-crime arrests of 45–50% during the studied interventions. Heller et al., Becoming a Man evaluations (NBER).

Why here.

Our first center is planned for the Arts District, connected to Boyle Heights. We want to turn that proximity into shared access: a place where East LA’s young people, families, and culture help shape the community, and where resources from across Los Angeles become part of everyday life.

Community

Life together.

One community.

Each class will share meals, train, build projects, explore the city, and work through challenges together.

Belonging and responsibility.

Belonging starts on day one.

You don’t have to have it together to belong here. We hold clear expectations, address harm, and help young men take responsibility and repair what they can.

Curriculum

A path toward leadership.

From belonging to leadership.

Each class will practice the habits that help a young man care for himself, keep his word, and become someone others can depend on. The journey starts with belonging and grows through responsibility, practical work, and service.

Five stages.

1. Belong and face reality.

  • Tell the truth.
  • Don’t let shame make your decisions.

Licensed therapists · Nutritious meals · The class

2. Build daily discipline.

  • Keep your responsibilities.
  • Control your appetites.
  • Accept correction.

Gyms, fitness, and wellness · Nutritious meals · Leadership and service projects

3. Take responsibility and repair.

  • Love people.
  • Repair what you’ve damaged.

Licensed therapists · The class · Leadership and service projects

4. Build your future.

  • Steward what remains.
  • Work diligently.

Financial experts and education · Entrepreneurs and working professionals · Gyms, fitness, and wellness

5. Pay it forward.

  • Lead through service. Use what you’ve learned for others.

Leadership and service projects · Field days across Los Angeles

Weekly experience.

A proposed first cohort of 12 young men meets once a week for three hours, with a meal and a brief mentor check-in.

TimeWhat happens
30 minutesArrive and connect. Shared meal and personal check-in.
45 minutesLearn a skill. A practical workshop led by a facilitator or guest.
75 minutesPut it to work. Practice, movement, a visit, or project work.
30 minutesReflect and commit. Discuss the experience and choose one next step.

Twelve weeks.

Build skills. Practice them. Carry them forward.

  1. Weeks 1–3. Belonging, identity, and responsibility.

    Establish trust, recognize strengths, and connect personal choices to the future each participant wants.

  2. Weeks 4–6. Money, work, and independence.

    Practice the skills needed to manage money and approach opportunities with confidence.

  3. Weeks 7–9. Health, relationships, and self-management.

    Develop useful routines and practice responding to stress and conflict.

  4. Weeks 10–12. Service, leadership, and next steps.

    Apply the cohort’s learning through shared responsibility and a concrete plan beyond graduation.

Graduation and beyond.

Each class will work toward graduation through shared experiences, practical learning, and service. Our goal is for graduates to leave with stronger relationships, useful life skills, and a next step toward education, work, or entrepreneurship—and, over time, to return as mentors the next class can turn to.

Principles

What we stand on.

Shared principles.

  1. Equal access and opportunity.

    Zip codes shouldn’t determine your access to opportunities.

  2. Leadership means service.

    We develop our abilities so we can care for others and contribute.

  3. We care for what we’re entrusted with.

    We take responsibility for people, resources, commitments, and the truth of what we report.

Faith foundation.

Our foundation is shaped by the teachings of Jesus: love your neighbor, tell the truth, practice mercy, take responsibility, and use what you have to serve others. These principles guide the adults as well as the young men in our community.
